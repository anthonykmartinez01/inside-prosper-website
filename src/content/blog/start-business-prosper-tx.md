## Step 1: Choose Your Business Structure

Before you file anything or spend money on permits, decide what type of business entity you're going to be. Your choice matters because it affects your taxes, personal liability, and paperwork requirements.

### LLC vs. DBA vs. Sole Proprietorship

The three most common structures for small businesses in Prosper are:

**Limited Liability Company (LLC)**
An LLC separates your personal assets from your business assets, which means if something goes wrong, your house and savings are generally protected. You'll file a Certificate of Formation with the Texas Secretary of State online through SOSDirect. This is the safest choice if you're investing your own capital or worried about liability.

**DBA (Assumed Name)**
A DBA, or "Doing Business As," is simpler and cheaper than an LLC. You can file it with Collin County Clerk's office for just $18.50. But here's the catch: it doesn't protect your personal assets. It's fine for freelancers or low-risk home businesses, but most business owners go with an LLC instead.

**Sole Proprietorship**
This is when you operate as yourself with no formal business entity. It's the simplest route, but it offers zero liability protection. You're personally responsible for everything.

For most people starting a business in Prosper, an LLC makes the most sense. You get protection and credibility without the complexity of a corporation.

## Step 2: Register with Collin County

<img src="/images/blog/start-biz-register.webp" alt="Business owner using laptop and phone for registration in Prosper TX" width="1200" height="675" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style="width:100%;height:auto;border-radius:12px;margin:1.5rem 0;" />

Texas doesn't require a general state business license, so you're registering at the county level.

### File Your Assumed Name or LLC Certificate

If you're forming an LLC, file your Certificate of Formation with the Texas Secretary of State at sos.texas.gov. You can do this online, and it costs around $300. If you're using a DBA or operating as a sole proprietor, file an Assumed Name Certificate with the Collin County Clerk's office. That's $18.50 for your first name, plus $1 per additional owner.

You can file in person at the Jack Hatchell Administration Building in McKinney or the Plano location. Email the Collin County Clerk at ctyclerks@collincountytx.gov or call (972) 548-4185 if you have questions.

Once you file, you've got your legal business registration sorted. That's step two, done.

## Step 3: Get Your Prosper Permits and Zoning Approval

This is where local rules kick in. Prosper has specific zoning districts and permit requirements, and you need to follow them before you open your doors.

### Check Your Zoning First

Before you sign a lease or buy property, contact the Prosper Planning Division and confirm that your business type is allowed in the location you're considering. Different zoning districts allow different uses. A restaurant won't work in a residential-only zone, and a home-based consulting business might not be allowed in some commercial districts.

The Planning Division can help you understand the Town's Zoning Ordinance and show you the online zoning map so you can see what's permitted where. Email planning@prospertx.gov to get started.

### Apply for Your Permits

Once zoning is confirmed, you'll need the right permits for your business:

**Building Permits**
Needed for any new construction, finish-outs, tenant improvements, remodels, or signage. If you're leasing commercial space and customizing it, you'll need a building permit before your contractor starts.

**Health Permits**
If you're selling, preparing, or serving food, you need a health permit and inspection. This goes for restaurants, catering businesses, food trucks, and bakeries. Check out our [food and dining directory](/food-and-fun/) to see who else is serving Prosper.

**Fire Permits**
Required for new construction, finish-outs, and remodels, plus any fire protection systems.

**Alcohol Permits**
If you serve or sell beer, wine, or spirits, get Prosper Town approval before applying for a state alcohol permit.

**Certificate of Occupancy**
You can't open to the public without one. It's issued once your space passes inspections and meets all codes.

Submit your applications online through Prosper's permitting portal at prospertx.gov. You can manage your projects 24/7 and track your progress from your phone. Contact the Building Inspections Division at permits@prospertx.gov if you need help.
